What is Shadow AI

The AI your IT team hasn’t approved, can’t see and can’t control.

Shadow AI is the use of AI tools, models, agents or features without the approval and governance of IT and security.

It is a subset of Shadow IT, but it moves faster: a single prompt can carry text, code and business context out of the company.

Banning it doesn’t work. Block access and usage simply moves to personal phones. The starting point is an honest inventory of what really runs on your devices.

Risks and vulnerabilities

Six gaps that turn productivity into exposure.

These are the same six dimensions the assessment below measures. The more of them are open, the more likely sensitive data leaves without anyone noticing.

  • R1 · Visibility

    You’re deciding in the dark

    Without an inventory of AI apps, extensions, models and agents on your devices, IT doesn’t know what to protect.

    If ignoredWhat isn’t in the inventory isn’t in any policy.

  • R2 · Data

    Data leaves with no log

    Prompts carry code, contracts, customer data and spreadsheets to services that may retain that content or use it to train models.

    If ignoredConfidential information outside the perimeter, untraceable and unrecoverable.

  • R3 · Governance

    Every team writes its own rules

    With no clear AI use policy and no approval workflow, each team decides for itself what is acceptable. Or decides nothing.

    If ignoredAudits and privacy obligations with no evidence to show.

  • R4 · Access

    The personal tool beats the official one

    Without SSO, conditional access and a catalogue of approved apps, a personal account is always the easier path.

    If ignoredApproved AI sits unused and unapproved AI becomes the default.

  • R5 · Platform

    Nobody owns the platform

    Device management without full admin access, enabled features or a specialist limits what you can control.

    If ignoredYou pay for capability you don’t use and don’t govern what matters.

  • R6 · Response

    Finding it isn’t enough

    With no automation or defined process, spotting an out-of-policy tool produces an email, not a fix.

    If ignoredThe problem stays open for days, or weeks.

    How Omnissa solves it

    Discover, assess, decide, enforce, educate. One cycle, one platform.

    New AI tools appear every day, so control has to be continuous. The Omnissa platform covers all five stages with Workspace ONE, Horizon and Elara.

    1. 01

      Discover

      Workspace ONE UEM inventories sanctioned and unsanctioned apps. Sensors find local models and services with no installer.

    2. 02

      Assess

      Omnissa Intelligence turns telemetry into a fleet-wide view. Each tool is classified: approve, approve with conditions, restrict or block.

    3. 03

      Decide

      Elara (beta waitlist) brings the signals together and applies one authority model to people, systems and agents. The AI Gateway sets who can reach which models.

    4. 04

      Enforce

      Conditional access, per-app Tunnel, Horizon and Freestyle Orchestrator turn the decision into technical control and remediation.

    5. 05

      Educate

      Intelligent Hub delivers a catalogue of approved AI. Banning doesn’t work. Offering a better alternative does.

    01

    Does Omnissa have a product called “Shadow AI”?

    Shadow AI is the problem, not a product. Omnissa addresses it with several capabilities: Workspace ONE UEM (inventory, sensors and conditional access), Intelligence, Horizon, Tunnel, Freestyle Orchestrator and Elara, the AI governance layer announced on 29 September 2026.

    02

    Is Elara available yet?

    Omnissa is accepting registrations for the Elara beta waitlist. Availability must be confirmed with Omnissa. You can start today with inventory, sensors, conditional access, Tunnel and remediation already exist in Workspace ONE. We assess how Elara could fit your environment when access and requirements are confirmed.

    03

    Why not simply ban AI tools?

    Because the usage doesn’t disappear, it moves. In Omnissa’s data, the great majority of AI use on enterprise iOS happens in unsanctioned apps, even where an official assistant has been provisioned. What works combines visibility, a better approved alternative and technical control over data.

    04

    Our Workspace ONE is managed by our carrier. Can you help?

    Yes. We’ll assess what you have, what is enabled and what moving to your own tenancy involves, side by side with an Intune migration. We can also work with your current provider through a transition, or take over administration.

    05

    Do we need to move to Microsoft Intune to detect Shadow AI?

    Not necessarily. According to Microsoft’s documentation, Shadow AI detection and blocking in Microsoft 365 is in public preview and, for now, applies only to Windows devices managed with Intune. For mixed fleets with iOS, Android, macOS, rugged and kiosk devices, Workspace ONE already covers the scenario. Sometimes moving is the right call, and we’ll tell you if it is.

    Microsoft documentation on Shadow AI.

    06

    Is the 6-step assessment an audit?

    No. It is an indicative score calculated only from your answers, in your browser. It doesn’t access your systems or data. The Shadow AI Health Check is the step that confirms everything with real information from your fleet.

    07

    Doesn’t blocking AI websites solve it?

    Only partly. Network blocks can’t see local models, extensions, AI features built into approved apps or personal devices. That is why the cycle starts with discovery on the device itself and ends with education and an approved alternative.

    08

    Do you only manage mobile devices?

    No. We work across Android, Apple and Windows with Workspace ONE, and with Omnissa Horizon for virtual desktops and apps, including extending the life of hardware you already own.
