Client use case · Field mobility

Field mobility at scale: 175 rugged tablets, delivered in half the planned time

Client profile
A large Australian utilityP01
Scope
175 rugged Android tablets for field work management
Platform
Omnissa Workspace ONE
Delivered
15 days against a 30-day plan

The situation

The client needed 175 rugged Android tablets built, configured and deployed to field crews working across multiple regional depots. The devices had to arrive fully locked down, running a controlled application set, with remote support, telemetry and location services all enforced by policy.

The engagement had a fixed deadline and a genuinely difficult starting position. Devices arrived from several suppliers in inconsistent states, some pre-registered with the manufacturer’s zero-touch enrolment programme and some not. Access to the management console and the asset system of record was not in place at kickoff. Several pieces of provisioning hardware were missing on day one.

SocialTech was engaged to build and deploy the fleet, and to leave the client with a repeatable process afterwards.

What we did

Built an assembly-line provisioning process. Rather than treating each device as a one-off build, we designed a repeatable production line covering asset registration, enrolment, configuration validation and quality check. Average build time landed at 45 minutes per device across the full fleet, including the devices that arrived without zero-touch registration and had to be brought into line manually.

Took ownership of the gaps rather than escalating them. Where prerequisites were missing, we sourced what was needed and kept the line moving. Two weeks were lost at the front of the project to access and onboarding delays that sat outside our control. The build still completed in 15 days against a 30-day plan.

Documented the process for the client’s own team. The engagement produced a working build instruction set and checklist, so subsequent tranches can be run in-house without re-learning the sequence.

Fed the lessons back. At closeout we delivered written feedback covering the prerequisites that would have removed most of the early friction: access provisioning ahead of mobilisation, consistent supplier build configuration, removal of single points of failure in vendor portal access, and provisioning hardware available on day one.

The finding that changed the conversation

Partway through the engagement, the client raised a platform question. They were evaluating a move to a competing management platform, driven primarily by licensing, since the alternative was already bundled in an enterprise agreement they held.P01

Our review identified a constraint that had not been factored into that decision.

On Android, seamless single sign-on for third-party applications depends on the application having a specific vendor authentication library compiled into it. Applications built that way sign in silently after the first credential entry. Applications that were not simply keep prompting, and no amount of policy configuration changes that, because the dependency sits inside the application code.

The client’s three core field applications, covering asset mapping, work order management and CRM, do not include that library. Under the proposed platform, field crews would authenticate to all three separately, and again whenever tokens expired.

Omnissa Workspace ONE resolves this differently. Workspace ONE Access authenticates the user from a certificate presented by the Tunnel client beneath the application layer, so the application vendor does not need to have done anything. The only requirement is support for SAML, which all three applications already have.

The commercial consequence was larger than the login experience. Because devices were being built before the end user was known, each one required a technician, a staging account and a restricted launcher, with manual steps throughout. Certificate-based sign-on requires named-user enrolment, and named-user enrolment removes the reason to pre-stage at all. Devices can ship sealed to the field worker, who powers on, enters credentials once, and finds every application already signed in.

Applied to a 175-device tranche, that is the difference between roughly 131 hours of build labour and roughly 29.

We presented this as platform-agnostic technical advice, with a written commitment to support the fleet either way. It was received as the most valuable output of the engagement.

Why this is a Workspace ONE story

Most management platforms can enrol a device and push an application. The difference shows up at the edges: certificate-based authentication that works regardless of whether an application vendor cooperated, remote support that can be granted by policy rather than by talking a field worker through consent prompts, and per-application network policy that makes silent authentication possible in the first place.

For organisations running field applications that were never built around a single vendor’s identity stack, that difference is the whole deployment model.
